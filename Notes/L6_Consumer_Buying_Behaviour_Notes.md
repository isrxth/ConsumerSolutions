## Topic 1: The Buying Behaviour S-O-R Model and Kano Satisfaction Analysis

### 1. Core Concept
**[[Consumer Buying Behaviour]]** represents the input-output transformation of sensory stimuli through the complex neuro-psychological processor of the "Consumer Black Box", resulting in quantifiable acquisition responses that are calibrated against dynamic [[Psychological Equilibrium]] baselines via the **[[Kano Model]]** [Slide 4, Slide 5, Slide 6, Slide 7].

### 2. Mechanism
* **The Stimulus-Organism-Response Loop [Slide 5]:**
  * *Stimuli (S):* External environmental, social, and product signals registered through the five human senses [Slide 5].
  * *Organism (O):* The Consumer Black Box—a complex, partially opaque neural processing engine combining biological, psychological, and cultural schemas [Slide 5].
  * *Response (R):* Observable consumption outcomes: what, where, when, and how much is consumed [Slide 4, Slide 5].
* **Kano Analysis Mechanics [Slide 6]:**
  * *Basic Attributes (Musts):* Essential baseline specifications. Their presence does not produce positive satisfaction, but their absence triggers acute dissatisfaction and frustration [Slide 6]. Maps to [[Enabler]]s.
  * *Performance Attributes (Linear):* Attributes whose value scales directly with execution speed, efficiency, or capacity; consumers demonstrate clear willingness to pay higher prices for superior performance [Slide 6].
  * *Excitement Attributes (Delighters):* Unexpected, novel features. Their presence creates genuine customer delight, while their absence causes zero dissatisfaction [Slide 6]. Maps to [[Differentiator]]s and [[Augmenter]]s.
* **The Relativity of Satisfaction:** Satisfaction is not a static state. It consists of multiple, constantly shifting psychological equilibriums that adjust relative to personal reference points and social comparisons [Slide 7].

### 3. Slide Evidence
* *SOR Engine:* "Stimuli include marketing and non-marketing stimuli a consumer gets exposed to through their sensors. Organism is through which the processing of those stimuli happens... Responses are the consumer's decision on a variety of consumption." [Slide 5]
* *Kano Categories:* "Basic attributes: Musts of the product. Presence cannot make customer satisfied. Absence or low performance will make the customer dissatisfied (e.g., Error free OS). Performance attributes: Customer satisfaction is proportional to the performance... Excitement attributes: Presence will make customer delighted... Highly noticeable (e.g., Voice commands in OS)." [Slide 6]
* *Relativity of Satisfaction:* "There is no single fabric of SATISFACTION that we can always wrap ourselves around. It has different equilibriums changing constantly depending on many factors." [Slide 7]

### 4. Exam Application (ITAR)
* **Issue:** A video editing software company releases an update with cutting-edge AI generative video features (delighters), but users flood forums with scathing 1-star reviews because the software crashes upon exporting 4K footage.
* **Theory:** **The [[Kano Model]] of Satisfaction** [Slide 6] and **[[Psychological Equilibrium]]** [Rules.md, Slide 7].
* **Application:** The company prioritized "Excitement Attributes" (generative AI tools) while neglecting "Basic Attributes" (crash-free file exporting) [Slide 6]. Basic attributes are non-negotiable [[Enabler]]s. When an enabler fails, it induces immediate neurological distress (disrupting equilibrium) that completely nullifies any pleasure derived from excitement attributes.
* **Recommendation:** Immediately patch core export stability to restore the baseline "Musts" [Slide 6]. Treat core engine stability as an uncompromisable enabler, and re-launch excitement features only once basic performance is rock-solid.

## Topic 2: Dual-Process Persuasion and Buying-Decision Topologies

### 1. Core Concept
Human cognitive processors route purchasing decisions through either analytical or heuristic pathways depending on their motivation and capability, categorizing buying actions into four behavioral quadrants dictated by involvement level and perceived brand differentiation [Slide 20, Slide 21, Slide 22].

### 2. Mechanism
* **Dual-Process Cognitive Routing [Slide 21]:**
  * *Central Route (High Motivation & Ability):* Slow, conscious, deliberate, analytical, rule-based, bottom-up processing. Scrutinizes technical facts, logic, and benchmark evidence [Slide 20, Slide 21].
  * *Peripheral Route (Low Motivation & Ability):* Fast, subconscious, automatic, heuristic, top-down processing. Relies on aesthetic cues, emotional valence, and authority heuristics [Slide 20, Slide 21].
* **The Four Buying Decision Quadrants [Slide 22]:**
  * *Complex Buying Behaviour (High Involvement + High Differences):* Deep cognitive investment and multi-stage evaluation (e.g., purchasing a primary workstation or enterprise server) [Slide 22].
  * *Dissonance-Reducing Buying Behaviour (High Involvement + Low Differences):* Expensive, risky purchase where available options appear functionally identical; triggers intense post-purchase anxiety requiring cognitive justification (e.g., purchasing a generic 4K panel) [Slide 22].
  * *Variety-Seeking Buying Behaviour (Low Involvement + High Differences):* Low monetary risk with frequent switching driven by curiosity and hedonic boredom rather than dissatisfaction (e.g., mobile games, snacks) [Slide 22].
  * *Habitual Buying Behaviour (Low Involvement + Low Differences):* Passive, automatic brand selection driven by convenience, default settings, and habit formation (e.g., default search engine or web browser) [Slide 22].

### 3. Slide Evidence
* *Processing Mind:* "High Motivation/Ability: Conscious, slow, deliberate, bottom-up processing, analytical, rule-base. Low Motivation/Ability: Automatic, subconscious, top-bottom, heuristic, effortless." [Slide 21]
* *Decision Matrix:* "High Involvement + Significant brand differences: Complex Buying Behaviour. High Involvement + Few differences: Dissonance Reducing Buying Behaviour. Low Involvement + Significant differences: Variety Seeking Buying Behaviour. Low Involvement + Few differences: Habitual Buying Behaviour." [Slide 22]

### 4. Exam Application (ITAR)
* **Issue:** An enterprise cloud security provider relies exclusively on humorous short-form cartoon advertisements on social media, yet B2B IT directors consistently pass over the service during procurement.
* **Theory:** **Dual-Process Persuasion** [Slide 21] and **[[Buying-Decision Behaviour]]** [Slide 22].
* **Application:** Purchasing enterprise cybersecurity represents **Complex Buying Behaviour** (high financial risk, profound professional liability, significant technical variance between providers) [Slide 22]. IT directors operate with high motivation and analytical capability, strictly utilizing the **Central Route** [Slide 21]. Relying solely on superficial, humorous peripheral cues signals a lack of technical rigor and fails to satisfy their analytical scrutiny.
* **Recommendation:** Restructure the marketing communication strategy around the Central Route. Publish detailed whitepapers, penetration test audit reports, SOC-2 compliance certifications, and live technical demos [Slide 20, Slide 21]. Reserve peripheral creative messaging for top-of-funnel brand awareness only.

## Topic 3: Product Diffusion, Adoption Stages, and Innovation Velocity

### 1. Core Concept
**[[Product Diffusion]]** is the population-level acceptance of an innovation triggered by individual **[[Disequilibration]]**, progressing through a 5-stage individual adoption funnel and distributed across a 5-tier adopter curve governed by five intrinsic product attributes [Slide 25, Slide 26, Slide 27, Slide 29].

### 2. Mechanism
* **The Catalytic Role of Disequilibration:** A consumer will not switch products while in psychological stasis. Consumption and switching require **disequilibration**—an induced dissonance or felt deprivation highlighting the inadequacy of their current solution [Slide 25].
* **5-Stage [[Product Adoption Process]] [Slide 26]:**
  * *Awareness:* Exposure to the existence of the solution.
  * *Interest:* Active gathering of specifications and data.
  * *Evaluation:* Cognitive calculation of value stack benefits vs. sacrifice costs.
  * *Trial:* Low-risk experimentation on a limited scale.
  * *Adoption:* Full behavioral integration into regular workflows.
* **The Diffusion Curve Tiers [Slide 27]:**
  * *Innovators (2.5%):* Wealthy risk-takers intrinsically motivated by new technology [Slide 27].
  * *Early Adopters (13.5%):* Educated visionaries and opinion leaders seeking strategic edge [Slide 27].
  * *Early Majority (34%):* Risk-averse pragmatists requiring peer proof and system stability [Slide 27].
  * *Late Majority (34%):* Skeptics who adopt only when pressured by market ubiquity [Slide 27].
  * *Laggards (16%):* Traditionalists adopting only after legacy alternatives cease to exist [Slide 27].
* **5 Determinants of Adoption Velocity [Slide 29]:**
  * *Relative Advantage:* Degree of perceived functional or psychological superiority.
  * *Compatibility:* Degree of alignment with existing user values, skills, and tools.
  * *Complexity:* Cognitive difficulty required to master and use the system.
  * *Divisibility:* Ability to experience the product without total upfront commitment (e.g., sandbox, trial).
  * *Communicability:* Ease with which results and prestige can be observed by peers.

### 3. Slide Evidence
* *Diffusion Catalyst:* "Diffusion is the acceptance of a product by the target consumers. Disequilibration: need this for consumption to trigger." [Slide 25]
* *Adoption Funnel:* "Awareness -> Interest -> Evaluation -> Trial -> Adoption." [Slide 26]
* *Adopter Segments:* "Innovators... Early Adopters... Early Majority... Late Majority... Laggards." [Slide 27]
* *Adoption Attributes:* "Relative Advantage... Compatibility... Complexity... Divisibility... Communicability." [Slide 29]

### 4. Exam Application (ITAR)
* **Issue:** A software firm develops a revolutionary graph-based IDE that compiles code 5x faster than VS Code, but developers refuse to switch from their traditional editors.
* **Theory:** **Adoption Velocity Attributes (Compatibility & Divisibility)** [Slide 29] and **[[Disequilibration]]** [Slide 25].
* **Application:** While the IDE possesses high **Relative Advantage** (5x speed), it suffers from severe **Incompatibility** (uses proprietary shortcuts, rejects VS Code extensions) and zero **Divisibility** (requires complete migration of existing projects) [Slide 29]. Developers' mental models remain in equilibrium with their existing tools; the psychic switching cost outweighs the perceived speed advantage.
* **Recommendation:** Engineer compatibility by supporting VS Code keybindings and existing extension architectures [Slide 29]. Introduce high divisibility through a lightweight CLI plugin or dual-editor mode that developers can trial on isolated files without migrating their entire workflow [Slide 26, Slide 29].
