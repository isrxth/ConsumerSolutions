## Topic 1: Service-Dominant (S-D) Logic vs. Goods-Dominant (G-D) Logic

### 1. Core Concept
**[[Service-Dominant (S-D) Logic]]** is a marketing paradigm stating that all economies are based on services (intangible knowledge, skills, and relationships), where physical products are merely facilitators of service delivery, and value is realized only during active use ("value-in-use") [Slide 15, Slide 16].

### 2. Mechanism
The transition from G-D logic to S-D logic changes how solutions are designed:
* **Shifting from Embedded to Co-Created Value:** Traditional engineering logic assumes value is built into the physical item (G-D logic). S-D logic asserts that value is co-created at the point of interaction and consumption [Slide 15, Slide 16].
* **Leveraging Intangible Assets:** The primary source of competitive advantage shifts from physical manufacturing assets to knowledge, skillsets, and relationships [Slide 16].
* **Objects as Service Vessels:** A physical commodity (like a laptop or smartphone) is viewed as a physical vessel designed to deliver services (connectivity, computation, entertainment) [Slide 16].

### 3. Slide Evidence
* *"Economy depends on service, not on products."* [Slide 16]
* *"Everything is a service. Even commodities are facilitators of services."* [Slide 16]
* *"Value is co-created at consumption [vs] Value is embedded in goods."* [Slide 16]
* *"Focus is on intangibles, such as knowledge, skills, and relationships... Knowledge is indirectly embedded in objects... Therefore, knowledge becomes the source of competitive advantage."* [Slide 16]

### 4. Exam Application (ITAR)
* **Issue:** A hardware firm manufactures high-end routers but struggles to compete with software-defined networking startups, despite the firm's superior physical components.
* **Theory:** **[[Service-Dominant (S-D) Logic]]** [Slide 16] and **[[Instrumental Value]]** [Rules.md].
* **Application:** The hardware firm is operating under traditional G-D logic, assuming value is embedded in the physical device's specifications [Slide 16]. However, consumers do not value the physical router itself; they value the service of stable, fast, and secure data routing. The startups are winning because they focus on intangible assets (software updates, security integrations, user experience).
* **Recommendation:** Pivot the company's solution architecture from selling "routers" to selling "connectivity-as-a-service". Bundle the hardware as a free or low-cost facilitator, and focus engineering efforts on software UI, on-demand integrations, and post-sale support to co-create value during active consumption [Slide 16].

## Topic 2: Value Co-Creation and Incremental Value Cycles

### 1. Core Concept
**[[Value Co-creation]]** is the collaborative process where value is dynamically realized through active interactions between the customer's cognitive context and the provider's resource architecture, occurring in **incremental value cycles** where previous value realizations set the stage for subsequent needs [Slide 17, Slide 18].

### 2. Mechanism
* **On-Set Value Realization:** Realization is experience-focused and depends on getting the customer involved in the ideation and development process [Slide 17].
* **Experience-Bound Needs:** Consumers cannot define their long-term requirements upfront because needs are discovered sequentially as they interact with the product [Slide 18].
* **Stage Setting:** Each realization of value alters the consumer's cognitive state, displacing their current equilibrium and establishing a new stage where the next set of values can be realized [Slide 18].
* **Architectural Shift:** The design organization moves from being a proprietary creator of value to a resource provider and experience enabler [Slide 17].

### 3. Slide Evidence
* *"Fundamental assumption: On set value realization... Focus on personalized consumer experiences through active interactions."* [Slide 17]
* *"Value co-creation is incremental. One set of values may set the stage for the other set of values to be realized. Without the stage, it is unlikely that the next set of values to be realized."* [Slide 18]
* *"This is why a consumer has no idea about what she needs. It is incremental and experience bound."* [Slide 18]
* *"What we manage is not the relationships, but the consumer EXPECTATIONS and FEELINGS."* [Slide 19]

### 4. Exam Application (ITAR)
* **Issue:** An enterprise software team holds long requirements-gathering meetings with customers, yet the final custom product is rejected by users because it doesn't solve their daily operational pain.
* **Theory:** **[[Value Co-creation]]** and **Incremental Cycles of Value** [Slide 17, Slide 18].
* **Application:** The team assumed clients knew exactly what they needed before using the system. Since value is incremental and experience-bound, the users could not accurately predict their cognitive needs in a vacuum [Slide 18]. The final software failed to fit their psychological equilibrium because it did not co-evolve with their actual user experience.
* **Recommendation:** Adopt an agile, co-creative delivery model. Deploy a basic functional product (enabler) to establish the first value stage [Slide 18]. Then, run active feedback loops (co-creation) to observe expectations and feelings, using each usage cycle to design and unlock the next layer of differentiators [Slide 17, Slide 19].

## Topic 3: The Value Delivery System and Consumption Ecosystem

### 1. Core Concept
The **[[Value Delivery System]]** is the multi-layered network of organizational and environmental nodes (including suppliers, trade partners, employees, and the ecosystem) that collectively enhance and deliver a solution, centered around the consumer as the ultimate source of value [Slide 21, Slide 22, Slide 25].

### 2. Mechanism
* **Distributed Value Generation:** Value is not created by a single firm in isolation; it is constantly added and modified as it flows through the delivery network [Slide 25].
* **Ecological Interdependence:** Consumption does not occur in a vacuum. It is heavily influenced by, and impacts, the surrounding natural and social environment [Slide 21].
* **Tri-Level Stakeholder Alignment:** To deliver value successfully, a design must satisfy the needs and maintain the well-being of the end customers, trade customers, and the internal team delivering the value [Slide 25].

### 3. Slide Evidence
* *"Every organization is a part of a large value delivery system. Value is not generated by single firm, but it is constantly being enhanced by each node of the network. Customer is the source of this network."* [Slide 25]
* *"Organizations should not only consider about end customers, but they should also consider about trade customers and the team..."* [Slide 25]
* *"Consumption occurs in a complex interactions of multi layered ecosystem... Protect your customer and the well-being of him/her through protecting the eco-system."* [Slide 21, Slide 22]

### 4. Exam Application (ITAR)
* **Issue:** An e-commerce delivery company has a highly optimized mobile app, but customers complain about late deliveries, rude delivery agents, and excessive packaging waste.
* **Theory:** **[[Value Delivery System]]** [Slide 25] and **[[Sacrifice Value]]** (specifically energy, psychic, and environmental sacrifice costs) [Rules.md, L4 Slide 17].
* **Application:** The organization focused purely on their direct digital product while ignoring the wider value delivery system. The digital value is completely destroyed by failures at other nodes (the delivery agents/logistics partners) and by causing negative externalities in the social/natural environment (packaging waste). This increases psychic and effort sacrifice costs for the customer, disrupting their psychological equilibrium.
* **Recommendation:** Re-architect the solution to manage the entire delivery ecosystem. Train delivery personnel as key nodes of the value network, optimize packaging materials to reduce environmental damage [Slide 22], and coordinate with trade sellers to align the entire supply chain, ensuring value is maintained at every interaction point [Slide 25].
