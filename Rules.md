## PART 1: THE THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK

### TOPIC 1: Market & Consumer Redefinition (The "Docker" Model)
* **The Market:** A market is not a physical place or demographic; it is a collection of similar cognitive and affect states.
* **The Consumer as a Container:** Inside a single consumer, multiple markets exist concurrently. The consumer is the "container" (or Docker) for all those distinct markets. 
* **Dynamic Construction:** Markets are constructed and deconstructed with every single consumption cycle. E.g., every bite of a pizza realizes the market, and the next bite is determined by a new realization.

### TOPIC 2: Mechanics of Consumption (S-O-R & Deprivation)
* **S-O-R Model (Stimulus-Organism-Response):** 
    * *Stimulus:* Five sensory inputs generated by a product/solution.
    * *Organism:* The consumer's psychological conditions and mechanisms.
    * *Response:* Psychological and biological responses that lead to a market response.
* **Deprivation & Equilibrium:**
    * *Psychological Equilibrium:* A hypothetical point where a balance of cognitions is achieved.
    * *Deprivation:* The felt dissonance caused by the gap between the "current state" and the "equilibrium".
    * *Consumption:* The process of reducing this felt deprivation by narrowing the gaps between the current affect states and the associated psychological equilibriums.
    * *Value:* The reduction in tension created by resolving the deprivation.
    * *Satisfaction:* The affect state felt by the consumer due to the realized value.

### TOPIC 3: The Tri-Core Apex of Consumption
Every consumption decision is driven by three foundational psychological anchors:
1. **Self-worth:** How the solution impacts the consumer's social identity or self-esteem.
2. **In control:** How the solution provides agency, predictability, and safety.
3. **Dissonance Reduction:** How the solution relieves specific acute mental tension or cognitive conflict.

### TOPIC 4: Value Classification & The Value Stack
Value is the released psychological tension resulting from consumption. It is categorized into a stack:
* **Instrumental/Utilitarian Value:** The product’s performance of expected functional characteristics.
* **Hedonic Value:** Sensory reception, emotional experience, and comfort created by the product.
* **Symbolic Value:** The psychological meaning associated with the product. This includes personal meaning (self-expression) and social meaning (prestige/what society thinks).
* **Sacrifice Value:** Minimizing the costs of consumption. This includes monetary cost, energy/effort cost, psychic cost (stress, perceived risk), and time cost.

### TOPIC 5: Solution Architecture & Decomposition (AVP Framework)
* **Product Definition:** A product is a "container of signals" (functional and aesthetic attributes) that a consumer interacts with, which is later realized as a set of values.
* **Attribute Mapping:** Attributes (e.g., UI design, hardware specs) act as sources of signals for the five senses, mapping directly to specific Value Elements.
* **Associated Value Profile (AVP):** Value elements are classified by their role in the market:
    * **Enablers (Primary):** Elements that keep the solution viable. They are why consumers "consider" the solution.
    * **Differentiators (Primary):** Elements that determine market realization and convert consumers from "considering" to "buying".
    * **Augmenters (Secondary):** Elements that enhance primary values and may become primary differentiators as the market matures.
